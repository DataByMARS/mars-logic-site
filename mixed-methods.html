Methods

When numbers need context

Mixed-methods work is strongest when quantitative and qualitative evidence are connected around a shared question—not merely placed beside one another.

Published by MARS

Modern Analytics & Research Solutions

Start with the reason for integration

Use multiple methods because the decision requires both pattern and explanation, breadth and depth, measurement and context, or confirmation from different forms of evidence.

Define what each method contributes

Quantitative evidence can show prevalence, difference, association, or change. Qualitative evidence can explain mechanisms, experiences, interpretations, and exceptions. State the contribution of each before collection begins.

Plan where integration occurs

Integration may occur in sampling, instrument design, analysis, interpretation, or reporting. The plan should specify how one source informs the other and how conflicting evidence will be handled.

Do not force agreement

Divergence is information. Differences between sources may reveal subgroup effects, measurement limitations, implementation variation, or assumptions that need to be reconsidered.

Report one coherent answer

A mixed-methods deliverable should not read like two unrelated studies. Organize findings around the research questions and show how each form of evidence changes the interpretation.
