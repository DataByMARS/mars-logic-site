Research Strategy

Before collecting data

A research project becomes easier to execute—and more useful—when five decisions are made before the first response, record, or observation is collected.

Published by MARS

Modern Analytics & Research Solutions

1. What decision must the work support?

A topic is not yet a research question. Start by identifying the decision, judgment, or planning need the evidence must inform. This establishes what “useful” means and prevents the project from expanding into questions that do not affect the intended decision.

2. What must be known to support that decision?

Convert the decision need into answerable research questions. Separate descriptive questions, comparison questions, relationship questions, prediction questions, and explanatory questions; each points toward different evidence and analytical methods.

3. What evidence can answer each question?

Define the constructs, measures, sources, and units of analysis. Decide what can be observed directly, what requires a proxy, and where multiple sources are needed to reduce blind spots.

4. How will the evidence be analyzed?

Specify the analysis before data collection whenever possible. This reveals whether the instrument, sample, timing, and data structure can support the intended comparisons or interpretations.

5. What will the final audience need?

Design the reporting format early. A technical report, executive brief, dashboard, or facilitated review requires different levels of detail and different visual choices. Delivery requirements should shape the workflow, not be added at the end.
